Sea and Sardinia

Retracing D.H. Lawrence, a century later

Santa Maria Navarrese and Cagliari, Sardinia · 24 June – 4 July 2023

“It is not that the island is of any great size. It is that it lies outside: outside the circuit of civilization.”

D.H. Lawrence · Sea and Sardinia

The rental car counter at Cagliari airport has two parties ahead of us. We are the third. Ninety minutes later we are still the third. The staff work with an unhurried gravity, each customer a small republic to be negotiated. When our turn comes they tell us that a debit card will not do. The reservation, paid in full, is beside the point. We push back. Things become briefly Italian in tone — voices raised, hands in the air. Hanna and I, it should be said, are not incapable of this register ourselves. Eventually a car is released to us. It is mid-afternoon. Whatever plans we had for the beach belong to a different day.

At the end of the week, filling the tank before returning the car, the attendant tells us the card reader is down. We hand him a note and he fills the tank. We drive off, and a kilometre later the fuel gauge is still at empty. He had mimed the whole thing. I feel both foolish and oddly respectful — the performance had been good.

Italy is like this. Things do not quite work. The country resists efficiency in a way that is part of its character — inseparable, I think, from everything one loves about it. The food, the villages, a stroll across a piazza with a gelato at four in the afternoon. You learn to love it as it is.

We drive north from Cagliari toward the beaches at Villasimius and Punta Molentis. The road curls through mountains that feel older than the rest of Italy. On a slope so steep it seems to defy gravity, a few goats are grazing, calm as if none of this were remarkable to them. The beaches, when we reach them, are wide and wind-blown, the water colder than we expected.

We had booked a larger boat with a toilet and lunch included; what we find at the quay is a rubber speedboat with neither. We buy sandwiches and climb in. The wind picks up. Hannes, ten years old, holds on with both hands and does not quite look up. Later, closer to Cala Goloritzé, we drop anchor and put on masks. The water is so clear that the bottom ten metres down seems no farther than an arm’s length, and in it, two white rays pass beneath us without hurry. On the ride back, Hannes is at the front of the boat, no longer holding on, grinning into the wind.

We start the hike around nine, walking down. The trail descends Supramonte’s slope through holm oak forest; the canopy holds us in shade and the air higher up still carries a breeze. In a clearing, a wild boar bursts from one side of the trail and disappears into the brush on the other before we have quite registered it. After ninety minutes the trees thin, and Cala Goloritzé appears below — a white half-circle of stones, an impossible turquoise, a limestone needle rising from the cliff. We jump the waves, big enough to push us around. Afterwards we eat the sandwiches we bought at Su Porteddu, the campground above, sitting on a flat rock in the shade. Mortadella and mozzarella. The salt of the sea is still on our skin.

Practical notes

We first stayed in Santa Maria Navarrese — a perfect base for exploring the Costa di Orosei, with its Blue Flag beaches. We then moved to Quartu Sant’Elena, just outside Cagliari; in hindsight we would have stayed in Villasimius. We reached Cala Goloritzé both by hiking and by boat. The boat day was long — eight hours, three beaches — and well worth it.
