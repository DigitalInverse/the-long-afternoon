On the Other Side

Three days on the western side of the Tiber

Rome, Lazio · 12-15 September 2014

“In Rome one had simply to sit still and feel.”

E. M. Forster · Where Angels Fear to Tread

We had both been to Rome before, and that changed everything. There was no list, no schedule, no morning that began with a museum. We had read in a travel magazine that Trastevere was a genuine quarter of the city, kept by the Romans for themselves, and we booked the Grand Hotel on the Gianicolo hill. The hotel had a pool and a garden and a view over the city.

The trip had a practical purpose, too. Hanna had lived in Terracina the year before, from August until April, and had left clothes and shoes there when she came back to Sweden. A friend brought them up to Rome for us. That was Wednesday.

So we did very little. We swam in the morning, in water that was always cold, and read by the pool through the afternoon, when the September sun warmed us back. In the evenings we crossed the river and walked, sometimes for hours, in the long warmth of a southern summer that had not yet noticed it was nearly autumn. We passed the Vatican but did not go in. I had been once, years before; Hanna never has. It was enough to see the dome of St Peter's at dusk, lit from below.

There is a gelateria artigianale a few steps from the Pantheon. It was nine in the evening. We had dark chocolate, possibly the best ice cream we have eaten anywhere. The piazza was full — Romans and tourists in the same warmth, the rotunda lit, the night holding. People have leaned against those same columns since the Pantheon was built in 126 AD. Standing there with our gelatos, we felt it.

On another evening we ordered a quattro stagioni at a place in Trastevere and the artichoke was missing from the artichoke quarter. They had run out. The waiter shrugged. So we ate the other three. That, too, is Italy.

The city is twenty-eight centuries old. The cobblestones are uneven, polished smooth by centuries of feet. The buildings lean a little, the way old buildings do. None of this matters once you have spent a few days inside it. We said we would be back. We have not, yet. One day with Hannes.

Practical notes

The Grand Hotel del Gianicolo is not a luxury hotel, but the position carries it: Trastevere below, the city across the river. Travelling with children, we would now choose an Airbnb — for the small comforts of a kitchen, a sofa. Next time, perhaps Testaccio? Like Trastevere it is a working-class quarter the city has not yet polished.
