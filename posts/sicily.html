Il Dolce Far Niente

The sweet art of doing nothing, in a Sicilian fishing village

Scopello, Sicily · 6–13 July 2022

“Bel far niente has always been a cherished Italian ideal. The beauty of doing nothing is the goal of all your work, the final accomplishment for which you are most highly congratulated. You don’t necessarily need to be rich to experience this.”

Elizabeth Gilbert · Eat, Pray, Love

Thirty-five degrees, and we are taking the shortcut up to Baglio Real Sito Borbonico, the fortified courtyard above Scopello — a dirt path that climbs the hillside without apology. The choice, as far as we can tell, is to keep moving and sweat, or to stop and sweat. We keep moving. When we reach the top, we take the shade beneath the old walls and the Tyrrhenian is far below us, still bright, still glittering, as if the sea has nothing else to do all day. We carry gelato limone out to the table. This, I think, is all the planning the week has required: the climb, the shade, the gelato. Life does not need to be much more than this.

Mornings begin on the terrace. Hanna practices yoga on the tiled floor, already warm in the early sun. Hannes draws in a notebook, or drifts between the hammock and the table. I have my moka — the small stovetop coffee that has nothing to do with the bars in town but everything to do with being at home somewhere. Below us, the bay begins to turn blue.

After breakfast we take the stairs down to Cala Mosca, the small cove below. Hannes looks for geckos on the way down. The water is warm, nearly empty; only a few other swimmers on the rocks. Hanna falls into conversation, in her uneven Italian, with an older woman from Milan, though Hanna seems to be laughing more than speaking. The woman tells us she comes back to Sicily every summer. To swim, to rest, to gather her strength for the year.

On the second morning I wake before the others with a fever — aching muscles, a sore throat. It is July of 2022, our first trip since the pandemic; I had picked up something on the plane, most likely. I take paracetamol and stand for a long time under the outdoor shower on the terrace, the same view of the bay below me, the warm water easing my muscles. Later we walk down to Cala Mosca together. The cove is only a few minutes away. I swim anyway. Whatever I have caught, the sea does not seem to mind.

Practical notes

We stayed in a house just outside Scopello, a ten-minute walk down to Cala Mosca. There’s a small alimentari in the baglio up the hill for the basics. For groceries and a wider selection, the drive to Castellammare del Golfo takes twenty minutes. The Tonnara di Scopello charges a small entrance fee but is worth it. The walk to Cala Capreria in the Riserva dello Zingaro takes about twenty minutes; the sun is unrelenting and there is no shade, but the cove rewards the effort. Bring plenty of water and something to eat.
