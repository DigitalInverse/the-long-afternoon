The Trip We Still Talk About

Praslin, Seychelles · 8–18 February 2015 · draft

Paragraph 1

We first saw Anse Lazio as a turquoise shimmer through the aeroplane window, passing over Praslin on the descent into Mahé. We had read about it in a magazine. The sand, it said, felt like vanilla mousse between your toes. We had eleven hours of flying behind us and a missed connection ahead of us, and from the air the beach looked like something that could not possibly be real.

Paragraph 2

By the time we got there it was already evening. Our luggage had not arrived. We swam in our underwear in water the temperature of a warm bath, with the sky going pink behind the granite headland. The guesthouse owner was in Australia; her niece and a friend looked after us. While we waited for our bags to be found and forwarded, Anders was given an ugly t-shirt and Hanna was given a beautiful sarong.

Paragraph 3

The guesthouse stood in a garden of its own, set back from the beach. In the mornings we ate on a terrace that looked out at the Indian Ocean, with land crabs at the edge of the grass and geckos on the walls. The fruit bats came out at dusk. We climbed up to the highest point on Praslin one day, near three hundred metres, through the kind of humidity that soaks a shirt before you have begun. A man who lived halfway up laughed at the state of us. On the way down we got a lift from a couple of Indian tourists.

Paragraph 4

We took the boat to La Digue on Hanna's birthday and rented bicycles. Grand Anse on the east coast — turquoise water, white sand, granite boulders worn round by the sea — would have been the most beautiful beach in the world if we had not already been staying at Anse Lazio. Petit Anse, just over the headland, was smaller and almost empty. We snorkelled and a curious surgeonfish bit Hanna on the thigh. On the west coast of La Digue was Anse Source d'Argent, famous from photographs and a little disappointing in person — too shallow to swim in, with seagrass on the bottom — but it was where we hid the key to a love-lock we had left on the Pont des Arts in Paris six months earlier. Three months after we left Seychelles, the city of Paris cut all the locks off the bridge.

Paragraph 5

We have talked about that trip a lot over the years. Partly it is the place — there are not many beaches in the world where the magazine writers turn out to have been telling the truth. Partly it is the timing. Hannes was born ten months after we came home. We did not know that yet, but the trip has the shape of a thing that happened before something else.

Paragraph 6

It rained on the last night. We woke to the sound of it on the roof, and were glad, because we were leaving in the morning anyway. We had a last swim in the ocean before the boat to the airport, and the Madagascar weavers came to the breakfast terrace for the last time, as they had every morning, looking for crumbs.

Things to discuss after reading

  1. General tone. Does anything stand out as wrong?
  2. Paragraph 5 — the Hannes mention. Keep or remove? It is the essay's retrospective anchor, but it is also more direct than other posts have been.
  3. Paragraph 4 — the Pont des Arts detail. Keep here, move to practical notes, or cut?
  4. "Shimmer" vs "mirage" for the opening — chose shimmer (more physical-optical) but the choice is open.
  5. The Vagabond quote (vanilla mousse) — kept because it is so specific. Sound right?
  6. Latin fish names — cut because too dictionary-like for essay tone. The biting surgeonfish remains as a moment.
  7. Petit Anse "almost empty" — I extrapolated this from the blog. If wrong, say so.
  8. Madagascar weavers as final image — small, specific, physical. Not grand. Works?
